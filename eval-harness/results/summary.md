## 7. Routing as an action

In auto-send mode the system decides what happens to a ticket: send the draft, hand it to a named human queue (the customer gets a holding reply, never the draft), or hold the draft for an agent. A draft that tells the customer to contact support from inside a support ticket is a *bounce*; the right action is an internal handoff. Expected actions and bounce labels: `reviews/actions.csv` (drafted by Claude, human sign-off 0/26).

*C3-gen* is the recommended configuration as designed. *Proposed* adds two inputs to the decision: the judge's `should_route_to_human` verdict becomes a handoff, and a detected bounce holds the draft.

## 8. Staging the rollout by detected intent

## 10. Release gates derived from tolerable harm

## 11. Shadow mode: where the gate evidence comes from

During agent-assist every draft still goes to a person, and the full quality layer runs alongside, logging what it *would* have done (`shadow/log_schema.json`). The reviewer's one-click severity tag is the label: a draft the configuration would have auto-sent but a reviewer had to change materially is an escaped failure. These records are real production traffic, which is what the constructed 26-ticket sample cannot provide.

**Demo only.** `results/shadow_demo_log.jsonl` replays the 26 sample tickets through this pipeline with the reviewer's action *simulated from the reference labels* (26/26 records synthetic). It proves the analysis runs end to end; it is not evidence. Replace it with real logs: `harness/shadow.py: load_log` + `analyze`.

| Measure | Demo value |

## 13. Caveats

- n = 26. A perfect catch of 6/6 release-blocking answers supports a catch rate of only about 61% or more (95% lower bound).
- The rubric, rules and reference labels were written after reading the sample. The judge prompt is generic and was frozen before any judge output was seen.
- The judge and the replay check answers against the full guide; a production guardrail would see only retrieved chunks, so these results are optimistic.
- Retrieved contexts are not logged, so retrieval failures cannot be separated from generation failures.
- Tier-1 uses TF-IDF (lexical); DoorDash used a semantic similarity check.
- The reference-label draft and the judge are the same model family; the judge ran in an isolated process without access to the labels. Human sign-off: 26/26 labels.
- Mean judge cost per call is lower on repeat runs because the system prompt and guide are prompt-cached.
- The held-out cases (section 6) were written by Claude after reading the rules, and their severities await human sign-off; injected faults are cleaner than natural ones, so held-out recall is an upper bound.
- The expected actions and bounce labels (section 7) are drafted by Claude and await human sign-off.
- The knowledge-base check (section 9) uses keyword retrieval and probes built from the failing tickets themselves.
- The shadow-mode numbers (section 11) are a demo with simulated reviewer actions, not evidence.
